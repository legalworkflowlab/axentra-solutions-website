AI governance needs operating controls, not only policies

Axentra Insights - 13 July 2026 - 6 min read

AI-enabled legal and business work becomes more reliable when teams define inputs, review points, ownership, escalation and delivery quality before scale.

AI governance often begins with policy language. Policy is useful, but operating teams also need a way to run the work. The operating question is how instructions, source material, prompts, review points and exceptions move through the workflow.

Controls need to sit inside the work

Useful controls include approved inputs, versioned playbooks, human review requirements, sampling routines, issue logs and escalation paths. These are the operating signals that show whether AI-enabled work can be relied on.

Human review remains part of the model

AI can assist classification, extraction, drafting and testing. It should not remove client judgement, professional review or business decision points. A managed workflow needs to identify what the tool can do, what the delivery team checks and what the client must decide.
